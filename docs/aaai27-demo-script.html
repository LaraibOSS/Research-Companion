Research Companion

A five-minute demo built around one claim: every single thing the tool shows you can tell you where it came from.

Deadline 18 Sep 2026, 11:59 AOE Video cap 5:00 Workspace recurrence · 16 papers Local 127.0.0.1:8799

Before you hit record

  • Dismiss the "Get set up" modal. It fires on load even though both keys read "Added". Load the page, click Maybe later, then start recording.
  • Pre-load the Graph tab once and leave it. Verified: the graph canvas runs at ~3 FPS and froze the renderer for 45s under inspection. Visit it before recording so the force layout has settled, and keep the on-camera dwell under ~15 seconds. Do not drag nodes.
  • The saved report topic is dataset used — weak on camera. Either regenerate the report with a real question or keep the shot on the coverage bars and citation chips rather than the topic line.
  • Pull the latest commit. The origin link rendered in the browser's default #0000EE (purple once followed) because its CSS never set a colour — nearly invisible on the dark ground, on the exact screen this demo builds to. Fixed in d608ae2. Recording without it would show the centrepiece looking broken.
  • Set browser zoom to 80%. Your display runs at 1.25 device pixel ratio; at 100% the cards crop awkwardly at 1536px.
  • Delete the note you create in rehearsal so the Notes tab starts with one legacy note, making the new one unmistakable.

The spine

Don't tour the tabs. Tabs are a feature list, and a feature list is what every other demo does. Follow one researcher's thread — a question, an answer, a gap, a note, and the way back — and let the tabs appear because the thread runs through them. The thesis that makes this an AI demo rather than a UI tour: grounding is worthless if it stops at the answer. This tool carries provenance all the way to the thing you wrote down at midnight three weeks later.

0:00 – 0:45  ·  The overview AAAI asks for

0:00Cold open — the pain45s

"A literature review means holding fifty papers in your head at once. Three weeks in, you have a folder of PDFs, a document of half-remembered claims, and no reliable way to answer the only question that matters: where did I get that from?"

"Research Companion is a local-first research environment that never loses that thread. It builds a knowledge graph over your own papers, answers questions with citations down to the section, surfaces the gaps your library is circling, and — the part I'll spend most of this demo on — remembers where you were standing when you wrote every note."

"It runs entirely on your machine. The core analysis is deterministic and needs no API key at all."

On screen
  • Home tab, already loaded, modal dismissed.
  • Let the two banners sit visible — they say 6 gaps in your library — 6 cited but not held and 2 papers need metadata. Don't explain them yet; they pay off at 3:30.

0:45 – 1:45  ·  A library that admits what it doesn't have

0:45Library — evidence strength, not a file list35s

"Sixteen papers on recurrent-depth language models. This isn't a file list — each card carries an evidence-strength badge computed from what we could actually extract: claims, results, datasets. Strong, moderate, weak."

"And note the filter called Needs you. That's papers the tool could not fetch on its own."

Click
  • Library in the left nav.
  • Hover one STRONG card, then click the Needs you filter chip.
1:20The honest-failure story25s

"When a PDF can't be fetched, most tools say 'failed'. We say why — blocked by the publisher, paywalled, no free copy exists — and whether a human can do anything about it. If your own browser can get it, the tool tells you so and watches your downloads folder for exactly that file, once, on a timer that expires."

"We never retry a 403. Retrying a block is indistinguishable from an attack, and it turns a host block into an IP ban."

Say only
  • No click needed if the filter is empty — the reason taxonomy is the point, not a populated list.

1:45 – 2:30  ·  Grounded answers

1:45Graph — the structure underneath15s

"Underneath is a typed graph — concepts, methods, datasets, claims and results, four hundred edges across sixteen papers. Retrieval runs over this, not over raw text, which is why answers can point at a section rather than a similarity score."

2:00Ask — every claim cites a section30s

"Ask a real question. The answer comes back with citation chips, and each chip goes to the paper and the section the claim came from. The scope is explicitly your library — never the open web. If your papers can't answer it, it says so instead of inventing something."

  • Click one citation chip to show it landing in the source section.

3:15Notes — and the way back15s

"Three weeks later that note is still here — and it still knows it came from a gap. Not which paper it was about. Where I was standing when I had the thought."

"One click, and I'm back at the exact gap, highlighted."

Do

  • Click it. The Gaps view opens scrolled to that row, outlined in blue. Verified: correct row, in viewport, outlined.

3:30 – 4:30  ·  Writing, and being checked

3:30Draft + Citations35s

"Point it at your draft and it scores every section against every paper — does this support you, challenge you, offer an alternative — with the supporting quote attached. It refuses to score from a bare title; if we don't have the text, we say we don't have the text."

"And it checks your citations: which references you cite but don't hold, and which are cited in the wrong place. Remember that banner from the opening — six cited but not held. That's this."

Click
  • Draft → scroll one alignment card. Then Citations → land on the misplaced flag.
4:05Report — cited, and honest about coverage25s

"A cited literature review over your own library: sub-questions, each answered by the same grounded engine, each with citation chips. And a coverage bar that tells you how much of the relevant material actually made it in — labelled as the BM25 heuristic it is, never dressed up as ground truth."

Click
  • Report. Keep the shot on coverage bars + chips, not the topic line.

4:30 – 5:00  ·  Close

4:30What's actually new here30s

"Grounded question answering isn't new. What we're demonstrating is that grounding shouldn't stop at the answer. The gap knows which papers raised it. The note knows which gap it came from. The alignment knows which quote supports it. The failure knows why it failed."

"It's local-first — your papers never leave your machine — the deterministic core runs with no API key, and it's open source. Come find us at the demo session."
